# Templates, tabs and writing habits

## Starting

The Welcome window (File › Welcome…) lists recent scripts and the templates by
category, with a few first steps. In Preferences you can have the app reopen
the scripts that were open when you last closed it, or skip the Welcome window.

**New from Template…** (Mod+Shift+O) starts from a screenplay, a TV episode, a
sitcom, a stage play, an audio drama, a treatment or series bible (text
documents for prose), or an outline in acts. **Save as Template…** keeps your
own; opening it starts a new script.

**Revert** puts back the script as last saved. A file you cannot write opens as
a copy, and Save asks where to keep it.

## Tabs and split views

Mod+T opens another script in a tab; Mod+W closes the tab (asking first when it
has unsaved changes). With more than one script open, a strip of tabs shows
above the script: click a tab to go to its script, the × on a tab to close it,
and + at the end of the strip for a new one. A dot after a name means unsaved
changes.

View › Split shows the index cards, scene view, story board, outline or the
script beside the main view, or below it. Drag the bar between the two to give
one more room; Swap Panes trades them.

## Looks

View › Extra Dark Theme is darker still than the dark theme. The status bar has
quick switches for focus mode and the theme, and a zoom slider from 75 % to
300 %.

## Writing statistics, goals and sprints

Tools › Writing Statistics shows your current and longest streaks, your best
day of the week and hours, and words, pages or minutes by day, week, month or
year with your goal as a line. Set goals in pages a day (on so many days a
week) and minutes a day. Writing time stops counting after a few idle minutes.
Statistics are kept on your computer, not in your scripts; turn them off in
Preferences.

Click **Sprint** in the status bar to write against the clock (25 minutes
unless you choose another length in Preferences); when it ends you see what you
wrote.

## Keys

File › Keys… lists every command with its key. Set… waits for the key you
press; Reset puts the command back. Your keys are kept with your preferences.
